# Rendering features

The renderer is built on [wgpu](https://wgpu.rs) and runs on Vulkan, Metal and DX12. A game never talks to the GPU directly. It describes what to draw, either as native world state that the engine extracts each frame (2D) or as a `RenderScene` (3D), and the renderer validates the description before allocating anything. An unsupported or out-of-range setting fails with a typed error at that point, before the first frame.

Each section below links to the developer page with the exact contract, for when you need units, ranges or ordering guarantees.

## 2D: sprites, tiles and UI

2D games draw from the native world. Each frame, the engine extracts sprites, tiles, UI text and the active camera, and draws them in the scene's color target.

- **Sprites and tiles** take a texture region, pivot, pixels-per-unit, linear RGBA tint, flips and a sorting layer and order. Sorting is stable: equal keys keep their submission order. Screen-space UI draws after world sprites with its own sorting.
- **Cameras** are orthographic. The enabled camera with the highest depth is drawn. Solid Color and Skybox clear to the background color, including alpha; a skybox material is not drawn. Depth Only and Nothing need a camera below them and are rejected.
- **Texture sampling** is set per texture: point, bilinear or trilinear filtering, and repeat, clamp or mirror wrapping on each axis independently.
- **Lit sprites** use global 2D lights that multiply sprite color per sorting layer, following URP's behavior for global lights. Point, spot and freeform lights and 2D shadows are not implemented.
- **UI text** is laid out natively from a Canvas and RectTransforms, rasterized from a TrueType font the game supplies, and drawn as sprites.

Contracts: [native sprites](../dev-docs/simulation/sprites.md), [2D textures and lighting](../dev-docs/rendering/2d.md), [UI layout and text](../dev-docs/simulation/ui.md).

## 3D scenes

A `RenderScene` holds meshes, materials, textures, object placements, optional terrain and an optional lighting environment. Scene coordinates are right-handed and Z-up, authored in integer millimetres.

- **Materials** are metallic-roughness, opaque or alpha-masked, with optional baked or vertex ambient occlusion, tint, wind animation and thin-surface transmission.
- **Meshes** can have several LOD levels with distance ceilings, and any level can be a camera-facing billboard card. Objects can be added, moved, hidden and removed every frame without re-uploading the scene.
- **Terrain** is a rectangular heightfield with blended materials. Large terrains can stream adaptive material pages based on screen-space detail.
- **Shadows** come from one directional light with one to four cascades, as fixed coverage boxes or camera-distance splits. Far cascades can refresh less often than the near one to save GPU time.
- **Water** bodies are rectangles, basin heightfields or authored meshes, with refraction, absorption, animated or texture-driven normals and flow, and environment reflections.
- **Sky** can be a procedural gradient with a sun disc, or an equirectangular dome texture, plus textured discs and meshes for moons and planets. Height fog applies to geometry, sky and water.
- **Image-based lighting** prefilters an environment cube map and a BRDF lookup table on the GPU for reflections.

Contracts: [scene materials and water](../dev-docs/rendering/scene-materials.md), [terrain material pages](../dev-docs/rendering/terrain-pages.md), [atmosphere and fog](../dev-docs/rendering/atmosphere.md), and the [render-scene README](../crates/render-scene/README.md) for LOD, instance editing and shadows.

## Frame profiles and post-processing

A renderer is built with one frame profile. The **legacy profile** shades straight to an sRGB target with one sample, which is enough for unlit 2D. The **HDR profile** renders 16-bit float radiance with 1x, 2x, 4x or 8x MSAA, then runs a post chain before writing the display image. These effects need the HDR profile:

- **Exposure**: fixed, or adaptive from a luminance histogram with separate rates for brightening and darkening. Capture modes can fix the exposure or the time step so output is reproducible.
- **Bloom**: a threshold with a soft knee, up to eight pyramid levels, using either a tent-filter pyramid or a separable Gaussian pyramid.
- **Tone mapping**: Reinhard.
- **FXAA** and **SMAA 1x**, which can be combined with MSAA. See [choosing antialiasing](#choosing-antialiasing).
- **Upscaling**: render at 50% to 100% of the output size and reconstruct with FSR 1 EASU or bilinear filtering, with optional RCAS sharpening.
- **SSAO**: hemisphere or spiral sampling with a depth-aware filter. It darkens ambient light only.
- **Screen-space reflections** on opaque surfaces and water, blended with the environment reflection by confidence and roughness.

Vignette, filmic tone curves, color grading beyond exposure, and sorted alpha-blended 3D geometry are not implemented. Requesting one fails with a typed error.

### Choosing antialiasing

One `AntiAliasing` value holds the choice: an `MsaaSamples` count (`X1`, `X2`, `X4` or `X8`) and a `PostResolve` (`None`, `Fxaa`, `Smaa`, `Cmaa2` or `Taa`) and optional CAS sharpening. Pass it to `FrameRenderer::hdr_antialiased`.

| Choice | Cost | What it smooths |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MSAA | Memory and fill rate grow with the count. | Geometry edges, using coverage samples. Shader aliasing and thin alpha-tested detail stay. |
| FXAA | One full-screen pass after tone mapping, reading an `Rgba16Float` intermediate (8 bytes a texel per frame slot). | Edges by contrast, including shader and texture aliasing, with some softening. |
| SMAA 1x | Three full-screen passes after tone mapping, plus two small intermediate targets. | Edges by contrast, blended by the shape of the edge. Near-horizontal, near-vertical and diagonal lines come out cleaner and thin lines stay crisper than with FXAA. |
| CMAA2 | A buffer clear, three compute passes and one full-screen pass after tone mapping, plus about 32 MB of storage at 1080p. | Edges by contrast, blended by shape, with extra care for text and thin detail. Needs compute shaders. |
| TAA | One full-screen pass and one texture copy before exposure metering, one `Rgba16Float` history texture, sub-pixel camera jitter and a depth contract (see [Temporal antialiasing](#temporal-antialiasing)). | Accumulates many jittered frames, so it smooths geometry, shader and texture aliasing and thin lines over time. Moving objects soften and can ghost slightly. |
| CAS (sharpening) | One full-screen pass after the resolve, plus one `Rgba16Float` intermediate. About 0.4 to 0.5 ms at 1920x1080 on a Radeon 780M. | Nothing is smoothed. It restores local contrast that FXAA, SMAA or a soft texture filter removed. |

MSAA 4x with no post resolve is the usual starting point. Add FXAA when the scene has specular or alpha-tested aliasing that coverage samples do not reach. 1x and 4x work on every adapter. 2x and 8x depend on the adapter and format: a count the adapter cannot render for `Rgba16Float` color and `Depth32Float` depth fails with `PostError::SampleCount` before any target is allocated. It is never reduced to a nearby count. Mesa lavapipe supports 1x, 4x and 8x and rejects 2x.

Prefer SMAA over FXAA when the image is dominated by long high-contrast edges (UI-like geometry, wireframe, fences, power lines, text in the world) and the extra passes fit the frame budget. SMAA reads clean stair-step shapes, so it works best on the geometry edges that remain after MSAA, or with no MSAA at all. It does not smooth texture or shader aliasing inside a surface, and it leaves a perfectly straight axis-aligned edge alone because there is no stair step to resolve. FXAA is cheaper and touches more of the image, which suits noisy specular detail. Use `PostResolve::smaa(SmaaPreset::Medium)` as a starting point. `High` adds diagonal and corner handling and `Ultra` searches further and lowers the edge threshold. `SmaaSettings` exposes the threshold, search distances, corner rounding and luma or color edge detection for custom tuning.

Choose CMAA2 (`PostResolve::cmaa2(Cmaa2Preset::High)`) when the scene mixes long edges with text or thin detail that must stay crisp and the adapter runs compute shaders. It is a port of Intel's CMAA2 2.3 single-sample path and works on the image after tone mapping, like FXAA and SMAA. The four presets differ only in the edge threshold (0.15, 0.10, 0.07 and 0.05, with `High` the default), and `Cmaa2Settings::with_extra_sharpness` keeps more small detail. On a Radeon 780M at 1920x1080 the CMAA2 passes took about 3.5 ms, SMAA 1.9 to 2.4 ms and FXAA 1.1 ms, so it is the most expensive of the three. Its MSAA path is not ported; the post chain receives the resolved image, so that path is not needed here. The shader is Apache-2.0 licensed; see the [post crate README](../crates/render-post/README.md#cmaa2) for the deviations and the license.

FXAA, SMAA and CMAA2 are alternatives, because `resolve` holds one choice. If the scene effects already author FXAA, leave `resolve` at `None`; setting both is rejected. `PostResolve::taa` is a fourth alternative and is described below.

Contrast adaptive sharpening is a separate setting, `AntiAliasing::sharpen`, and works with any resolve: `AntiAliasing::new(MsaaSamples::X4, PostResolve::fxaa(1.0)).with_sharpen(CasSettings::new(0.5))`. It is AMD FidelityFX CAS in its no-scaling form. For each pixel it compares the pixel with its four neighbours and sharpens where there is headroom to black and white, so flat areas and edges that already reach black or white are left alone. `sharpness` runs from 0 (the reference default, least ringing, still sharpens) to 1 (strongest). It is off by default and then costs nothing. Use it after FXAA or SMAA, which soften fine detail, or at native resolution when the image looks soft. It runs at the render extent before any upscale. RCAS, the sharpening pass of the FSR 1 upscale, is a different stage: it runs at the output extent after reconstruction, is set with `UpscaleSettings::sharpness` in stops and exists only when an upscale is configured. Use RCAS to recover detail lost by upscaling and CAS to offset the softness of the antialiasing resolve. Both can be on, but check that the combination does not over-sharpen.

### Temporal antialiasing

`PostResolve::taa(0.9)` or `PostResolve::taa_with(TaaSettings { .. })` accumulates frames. Each frame the projection moves by a sub-pixel offset from a Halton(2,3) sequence. The resolve rebuilds the pixel from the jittered samples around it, reprojects the previous result with the camera motion and a depth, clips that history to the colors of the current neighborhood and blends the two. Over about ten frames a static edge converges toward a supersampled one. The technique follows Brian Karis, "High Quality Temporal Supersampling" (SIGGRAPH 2014).

Settings, both finite and range checked by `TaaSettings::validate`:

| Field | Range | Default | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `history_weight` | 0.0 to 0.99 | 0.9 | Share of the reprojected history in the blend. Higher is smoother and slower to follow change. |
| `clip_scale` | 0.5 to 2.0 | 1.0 | Scale of the min/max color box of the 3x3 neighborhood, about its centre. Smaller rejects history sooner and ghosts less. |

The scene recipe `SceneAntiAlias::Taa { history_micro, clip_scale_micro }` maps to the same resolve: `history_micro` is `history_weight` in micro-units (0 to 990_000) and `clip_scale_micro` is `clip_scale` in micro-units (`500_000..=2_000_000`, validated when the effects are built). `SceneAntiAlias::taa(history_micro)` keeps the engine default clip scale of `1_000_000`. The recipe is programmatic only; the JSON v4 `hdr_post` block has no TAA field. Jitter is automatic: a TAA resolve selects `ProjectionJitter::default()` unless `AntiAliasing::with_jitter` chose another length, and `FrameRenderer::set_projection_jitter(None)` returns an error while TAA is resident. TAA is one resolve, so it excludes FXAA, SMAA and CMAA2.

The pass runs before exposure metering, bloom and tone mapping, on the scene-referred `Rgba16Float` image. History therefore holds no exposure, so an adaptive exposure change cannot ghost, and metering and bloom see the accumulated image instead of a shimmering jittered one. The color box uses luminance-compressed values so a bright pixel cannot dominate the clip, and the blend is linear because luminance weights pulled every edge toward its darker side. The current frame is reconstructed from the sample nearest the pixel centre, so the jittered frames accumulate to a box-filtered pixel instead of a blurred one. MSAA works with TAA: the hardware resolve runs first, and with 4x or 8x samples the depth used for reprojection is the per-sample minimum. A multisampled frame is not jittered (`ProjectionJitter::offset_pixels_for` returns zero for more than one sample): its pixels are already box-filtered area averages, and accumulating those at shifted positions converges to the box convolved with the jitter distribution, a tent twice as wide as a pixel. Resampling the shifted frame at `p + 0.5 + jitter` with Catmull-Rom before accumulating did not recover the box (static 4x error 0.00153 against 0.00046 for MSAA alone). TAA on MSAA input therefore stabilises over time and adds no spatial supersampling; a static 4x image equals the MSAA image.

`ProjectionJitter::for_extents(render, output)` builds the sequence for a scene rasterised below its output extent. The length is `ceil(8 * max(Ow / Rw, Oh / Rh)^2)`, computed per axis with exact integer arithmetic from the rounded extents: 8 phases at equal extents, 32 at a render scale of 0.5, 18 at 0.67 and 14 at 0.77 (the same for 1920x1080 and 1452x921). Offsets are in internal raster pixels, and the projection shift divides them by the internal extent. It rejects empty extents, a render extent larger than the output on either axis, a ratio below 0.5 (the `RenderScale` limit) and a length above 64. Native TAA and TAA with spatial upscaling keep the 8-phase default unless a caller passes the result to `AntiAliasing::with_jitter`; `UpscaleMode::Temporal` selects it by itself (see [Temporal upscaling](#temporal-upscaling)).

Depth contract: draw the scene into `FrameRenderer::targets().depth()`. The resolve reads that texture, and `encode_scene` refuses another depth attachment while TAA is resident. Call `FrameRenderer::reset_temporal_history()` on a camera cut. History also resets on the first frame, on resize, when the jitter changes and after a cancelled or failed frame. A reset frame equals the first frame of a fresh renderer.

Limits of this release:

- Wind, instance motion and the motion of camera-facing cards are produced. Scenes drawn with `ScenePipelines::with_taa_velocity` write the residual of an object's wind deformation and of its own transform change (opaque, masked and relief) into the velocity target, and the resolve adds it to the camera reprojection. Each batch keeps a previous-transform buffer beside its instance buffer while a pass draws velocity pipelines: a frame that edits a slot (`set_instance_transform`) first writes that slot's last published transform there, a slot left alone for one frame converges to previous equal to current, and new or reused slots start equal to current. A cancelled frame leaves the buffer as the last published frame had it. Velocity pipelines bind it as a third per-instance vertex buffer at locations 12 to 15 (instance layout and stride; only the matrix columns are read); shadow casters and non-TAA pipelines keep two buffers. A yaw-locked or spherical billboard and an impostor card build their basis from the camera, so their previous vertex position is rebuilt from the previous camera position, the previous pivot and scale and the previous clock, through the same basis helper as the current frame: a card that turns toward an orbiting camera, or whose instance moves, writes the world motion of its vertices, and an unchanged camera, instance and clock write exactly zero. Relief impostors use the same cropped plane point for both frames. An impostor's atlas frame switches with the view direction; that changes the card's appearance, not its position, so it is not motion and stays with the neighborhood clip. Crossed-quad cards do not turn and follow the instance transform like any fixed mesh. Terrain, water, decals, dynamic quads, sky and celestial meshes keep a zero residual. Shadow receivers built with `ShadowedScene::with_taa_velocity` write the same residual (caster depth draws have no colour target). With SSR on, the reflection forward draws write it as a fourth colour target beside the three reflection targets when the device allows 28 bytes per sample (`max_color_attachment_bytes_per_sample`, 32 by default); on a device with a lower limit they keep the camera reprojection. SSAO without SSR writes it through `ScenePipelines::with_ssao_taa_velocity` (the SSAO metadata prepass itself writes no velocity); `ScenePipelines::with_ssao` keeps the camera reprojection. With 4x MSAA the velocity attachment stays multisampled and the resolve reads the minimum-depth sample; the gain from wind velocity is small there because MSAA is not jittered. At 1x the resolve takes the camera reprojection and the residual of the closest-depth texel in the 3x3 neighborhood (Karis dilation). At 4x it does not dilate: both come from the minimum-depth sample of the pixel itself, because the samples already resolve the edge and a neighbor's residual handed to a background pixel cancelled the card's camera reprojection, so that pixel kept history with card colour. On an orbiting camera-facing card at 4x (64x64, 12 frames, lavapipe, mean 8-bit error against the unjittered frame), the whole-band error with the previous camera basis went from 0.002897 with the dilated residual to 0.000895 with the pixel's own residual and 0.000428 with no dilation; the moving-instance 4x band error went from 0.00988 to 0.00028 (0.00321 without velocity). The camera-only frame (`without`) equals the plain MSAA frame because 16 times the card's 0.8 px camera motion zeroes its history weight, so a whole-band comparison against it measures history lag. The test splits the error by region: the background (0.0 with and without, away from and beside the card) and the card (0.00135 with, 0.00005 without, under one 8-bit step of 0.0039). The edge pixels of the card carry most of the lag (0.0037 against 0.0005 in the interior, measured before pooling).
- Sky and other pixels at depth 1.0 reproject approximately.
- SSAO and SSR run on the jittered matrix. Measured on lavapipe at 64x64 with a fixed camera over 16 frames (frames 8 to 15, 8-bit sRGB values on a 0 to 1 scale), this adds no visible flicker and no bias at 1x or 4x. Frame-to-frame change of the resolved image is 0.00073 with SSAO against 0.00072 without it, and 0.00059 with SSR against 0.00057 without it. At 4x the jitter is zero, so the output is static (0.0). SSAO moves by 0.00047 and SSR by 0.00011 between TAA on and off, against feature contributions of 0.0093 and 0.0068, so the prepass and the reflection rays keep the jittered matrix. The tests are in `crates/render-scene/src/draw/tests/taa_screen_space_tests.rs`. The scene is one creased floor and card, so a thin or high-frequency receiver can still shimmer; no such case was measured.
- History is read with a Catmull-Rom filter (nine bilinear taps, negative lobes clamped to zero) and blended with a motion-adaptive weight: the history weight is scaled by `clamp(1 - k * offset, 0, 1)`, where `offset` is the pixel's reprojection displacement in pixels, with k = 1.5 for jittered 1x frames and k = 16 for multisampled frames. A static pixel keeps the full weight. The trade-off is that a moving pixel supersamples less: at 1x, TAA in motion is only 0.73x to 0.88x the error of a history-free frame, because the jitter accumulation needs frames at the same scene position, and a multisampled image in motion is the MSAA image with history only on pixels that move less than 1/16 px per frame. Measurements showed that the earlier loss did not grow with the number of resampled frames (the error was at its plateau from the first moving frame); the likely cause is that edge pixels take the closest neighbor's motion and thin features resample poorly, errors the weight avoids by trusting history less where there is motion (not isolated further). Motion-weight sweep at 1x (same scene, ratio of TAA to history-reset error, +41 mm / -29 mm): k = 0 gives 1.009 / 0.888, 0.5 gives 0.918 / 0.813, 1.0 gives 0.886 / 0.761, 1.5 gives 0.883 / 0.732, 2 gives 0.891 / 0.717, 2.5 gives 0.906 / 0.736 and 3 gives 0.933 / 0.771. Using only the sub-pixel fractional part of the offset gives the same numbers at k = 1.5 and 3, because the camera paths move the nearest object 0.27 px per frame, below the half pixel where the fractional part differs from the length. Quadratic and squared-linear falloffs reached at best 0.880 / 0.714. Swaying foliage under the object residual (error against the unjittered frame, 0.014172 without velocity) measures 0.011956 at k = 0, 0.008500 at 0.5 and 0.008560 from 1.0 to 3, so object velocity already removes the sway loss and k does not matter there. No variant reaches the 0.85x target on the +41 mm path: the ratio bottoms out at about 0.88 for k between 1.0 and 1.5, and k = 0 is worse than a reset frame. The remaining loss is not set by the weight (its cause, history resampling or edge selection, is still not isolated), so the weight is unchanged.
- A multisampled frame is not jittered, so an explicit `AntiAliasing::with_jitter` sequence on a multisampled target has no effect: `ProjectionJitter::offset_pixels_for` returns zero for more than one sample.
- A depth history rejects history that belonged to another surface. The resolve stores the linear view depth of each pixel's closest-depth texel (`R32Float`, a second target of `taa resolve`, copied by `taa depth history`) and, for the next frame, predicts the depth the history sample should have from the reprojection. History is dropped (the pixel takes the current color) when the prediction lies outside the stored depths of the 2x2 footprint at the history position, widened by 2 percent. The interval, rather than the minimum, keeps steep surfaces at grazing angles from rejecting themselves. The test runs only on pixels with a zero object-motion residual: the residual is XY only, so a moving object that changes its view depth would fail falsely, and such pixels still rely on the color clip. It needs a perspective projection with standard Z; an orthographic camera or a singular one disables it. Sky is predicted at the far plane and is not rejected against sky. The linearisation is read off the camera's own unjittered view-projection (the camera crate uses 0..1 depth, not reversed, with a finite far plane). In a test where a bar sweeps over a checkerboard whose range contains the bar colour, so the colour clip accepts the stale bar, the mean error in the uncovered strip is 0.00024 with the test and 0.180 without it on the GPU (0 and 0.180 in the f64 reference); the tests require a factor of 100 on the GPU and 10 in the reference. Under camera motion the test changes nothing measurable: the 1x motion ratios stay 0.883 and 0.732, and swaying foliage with velocity stays at 0.00856, while the same foliage without velocity improves from 0.01417 to 0.01251. Cost at 1920x1080 on a Radeon 780M: `taa resolve` is 1.99 and 2.01 ms (2.04 and 1.93 ms before the depth history) and the untimed whole chain is 3.99 and 4.18 ms per frame (3.78 and 3.77 ms before), so the second target and its copy add 0.2 to 0.4 ms per frame.

Measured on lavapipe at 64x64 against a 4x supersampled reference, after 32 static frames: mean error 0.0020 (1x) and 0.00046 (4x MSAA, unjittered) on 8-bit sRGB values, against 0.0044 for one unantialiased frame and 0.00046 for 4x MSAA alone. In motion (16 frames, +41 mm per frame, about 0.27 px on the near object), the mean error over frames 4 to 15 at 1x is 0.00230 for TAA against 0.00260 when history is reset every frame (0.88x, 0.0031 against 0.0033 on the last frame); on the -29 mm path it is 0.00185 against 0.00254 (0.73x). At 4x the TAA last frame measures 0.00036 (+41 mm) and 0.00043 (-29 mm), equal to 4x MSAA alone, from 0.0027 before the motion weight (7.7x MSAA alone). Diagnosis at 4x before the change: history weight 0.9 gave 0.0025, 0.5 gave 0.0009 and 0.0 equalled MSAA alone (0.0004), and the error was flat from the first moving frame. A second camera path moving the other way keeps the edge within half a pixel at 1x and 4x. The tests are in `crates/render-scene/src/compositor/taa_end_to_end_tests.rs`. The history copy is a texture copy shared by all frame slots, about 0.4 ms at 1920x1080 on a Radeon 780M against 0.9 ms for a fullscreen copy pass, and a test reuses slot 0 every frame to check the history does not depend on slot order. Details of the passes are in the [post crate README](../crates/render-post/README.md#temporal-antialiasing) and [render-scene README](../crates/render-scene/README.md#temporal-antialiasing).

### Temporal upscaling

`UpscaleSettings { mode: UpscaleMode::Temporal, sharpness }` reconstructs the output image from jittered frames rendered below the output extent. Use it when the scene is mostly static or slow, TAA is already on, and the render scale is 0.5 to 0.77: the accumulated frames recover edges that one frame at that scale cannot hold. For fast motion or MSAA keep `Easu`, which needs no history.

Requirements, each rejected with a typed `PostError::UnsupportedProfile` before any allocation: the HDR profile, a TAA resolve (`PostResolve::taa`), one sample per pixel, a render extent of at least half the output on each axis, and no standalone `SpatialUpscale`. At equal extents `Temporal` is the native TAA path.

The projection jitter comes from `ProjectionJitter::for_extents(render, output)`: `ceil(8 * max(Ow / Rw, Oh / Rh)^2)` phases (32 at 0.5, 18 at 0.67, 14 at 0.77), offsets in internal raster pixels. `FrameRenderer::hdr_antialiased` installs it. A renderer built from a `RendererConfiguration` (`ConfiguredFrameRenderer`) installs no jitter on its own, for native TAA as well, and has no public setter for it; the end-to-end test reaches the inner renderer from inside the crate. Closing that gap is required before a host that uses `RendererConfiguration` can run `Temporal`. An explicit sequence shorter than the derived one is rejected. Output pixel `p` reads the current frame at `q = (p + 0.5) / O * R + jitter - 0.5` with a normalised Lanczos2 kernel, reads the Catmull-Rom history on the output grid, clips it in YCoCg to the 3x3 neighbourhood and blends. Exposure metering, bloom, tone mapping, CAS and RCAS then run at the output extent; `UpscaleSettings::sharpness` becomes a display-linear RCAS after reconstruction. History is keyed by both extents: a change of either one, the first frame, a camera cut, a settings change and a cancelled or failed frame reset it, and a reset frame equals the first frame of a fresh renderer.

Measured ratios, temporal error over reset-every-frame error (the same pipeline with the history discarded before each frame), lavapipe, static scene, zero camera motion, mean absolute error over one full jitter cycle after one warm-up cycle:

| Render scale | Render-post harness, 160x96, analytic truth | Render-scene `FrameRenderer`, 64x64, 4x supersampled reference |
|---|---|---|
| 0.50 | 0.62 | 0.39 |
| 0.67 | 0.52 | 0.80 |
| 0.77 | 0.45 | 0.66 |

The design bound is 0.90, and both tests assert it. The gain sits on edges (edge pixels carry about 90% of the error); on the smooth interior the render-post harness measured temporal worse than reset at 0.67 and 0.77 (ratio 1.12 and 1.32, absolute error about 0.002). A single EASU frame is not beaten at 0.5 in that harness (0.0237 against 0.0250). The table above is static. Motion and disocclusion are measured in [Camera motion, dilation and depth clip](#camera-motion-dilation-and-depth-clip); colour is not measured. The history weight falls with motion as for native TAA. The tests are `crates/render-post/src/temporal_quality_tests.rs` and `crates/render-scene/src/compositor/temporal_upscale_end_to_end_tests.rs`.

#### Camera motion, dilation and depth clip

The temporal stage reprojects history with the total backward motion of each texel: the camera term plus the object residual that `velocity.rs` writes. Three compute stages at the internal extent run ahead of `temporal upscale` in every slot, and `taa_reconstruct.wgsl` holds all of them.

* **Camera term.** `TemporalCamera` (80 bytes, its own per-slot uniform) carries the f64 `previous * inverse(current)` reprojection of the unjittered cameras, the same producer native TAA uses, uploaded as f32. The dilate stage turns the dilated depth of a texel into its position one frame ago. An unusable camera uploads zeros and means "no camera term", which is what a slot holds before its first upload.
* **Dilation.** Each texel takes the depth and residual of the nearest valid depth in its 3x3 neighbourhood (ties go to the first texel in raster order) and writes them to the dilated depth and dilated motion planes. A moving silhouette therefore hands its motion to the texels one step outside its edge. Valid means `0 < z < 1`: a cleared depth, sky, a negative or a non-finite value is not a candidate.
* **Depth clip.** The dilate stage also scatters its dilated depth to `s + motion * R` with `atomicMin` over the 2x2 footprint into a reconstructed previous depth buffer. The clip compares the linear depth of a texel with the nearest depth that reached the texel its history position falls on. It writes confidence exactly 0 or 1: 0 when the two differ by more than the largest step to a 3x3 neighbour plus `2^-20` of the linear depth (f32 and raster precision), or when the history position is outside the frame or nothing reached it. `temporal upscale` takes the minimum confidence of the 2x2 texels around its sample position and scales the history weight by it, so confidence 0 gives the current Lanczos2 reconstruction exactly.

A texel without a valid depth (sky, cleared) has confidence 1, and so has every texel when the camera block carries no depth terms: nothing there says the history is wrong, and the YCoCg clip still applies. The residual stays XY only, so a surface that moves along the view axis is reconstructed with its current depth as its previous depth; the clip then costs it history at the edge where its depth changes (see the numbers below) and never accepts the wrong surface for the screen-plane part of the motion.

Resources per slot at the internal extent R: dilated depth (R32Float), dilated motion (Rg32Float), confidence (R32Float) and the reconstructed depth buffer (4R bytes), 20R bytes in all. They are per generation entries of the allocation set, not shared, because a resize builds the new bank while the retiring generation still holds the old one. Rg16Float and R16Float would halve two of the planes, but a single or double channel 16 bit float storage texture is not a core format.

**Thin-feature locks (implemented, shipped off, no measured gain).** The locks do not improve the output in any measured configuration, so `LOCKS_DEFAULT` in `taa_upscale_gpu` is `false` and the default output is byte for byte the output without them (the thin-feature sweep asserts `to_bits` equality of the default chain and the `WithoutLocks` chain on all 15 fixture and speed cases at each of the three scales). Enabling them is tracked in issue #436.

With locks off, the default and `WithoutLocks`, the chain schedules no `temporal create locks` and no `temporal lock history`, writes no lock bank and allocates no candidates, lock bank or lock history: `temporal upscale` runs its `taa_upscale_unlocked` entry point with one target and without the lock bindings. The pipeline override `locks` still gates the lock branches of the locked entry point. Everything below describes the path with locks on (`WithLocks`, test only).

Pipeline with locks on. `temporal create locks` (`taa_thin.wgsl`, compute at the internal extent R) marks one texel features: the luminance `y / (1 + y)` of a texel differs from both neighbours on one of four axes by at least 1/16 while the two neighbours agree within a quarter of the smaller contrast. It writes `Rg32Float` (strength, luminance). `temporal upscale` gathers the strongest candidate of the 2x2 texels around its sample (0 at confidence 0 and at nonfinite motion), reprojects the per-slot `Rgba16Float` lock bank at the output extent O with the total motion (exact texel load), and writes the new bank beside the colour. The bank holds frames left, reference luminance, contrast and one unused lane. A lock lives one jitter cycle (`phase_count` frames) after its last candidate and is killed by an unusable history (reset, outside the frame), confidence 0, or a one frame jump of the 3x3 YCoCg box centre larger than the stored contrast. A locked texel raises its history weight from `w` to `1 - (1 - w) / 2`; `history_weight == 0` stays 0. `temporal lock history` copies the bank to the shared history.

Resources with locks on, measured by the real-`StageSet` allocation test (with locks off none of them is allocated and the same test shows no lock bytes): candidates 8R per slot, lock bank 8O per slot and one shared lock history 8O, so 24R + 32O bytes for three slots, only when the locks are enabled. The design counted 16O (two 16 bit channels at 4O per image). A single or two channel 16 bit float storage texture is not a core format, so the candidates use `Rg32Float` and the bank uses the four channel `Rgba16Float` that the output already uses. At 160x96 with R 80x48 that is 24 * 3840 + 32 * 15360 = 583,680 bytes.

Rules, fixed by the coordinator before any lock code. Rule 1: on 18 designated cases (the masked grid, wire 45 and wire 90, static and at 0.3 px per frame, scales 0.5, 0.67 and 0.77) the ROI error with locks is at most 0.90 times the error of the same chain without locks on the same device. Rule 2: on the other 27 cases (wire 0 and wire 30, and speed 0.1) at most 1.10 times. Rule 3: a vanished line (wire 30 and grid) is within 2/255 display levels of the line-free truth after at most two submitted frames. The ROI is the set of output pixels within 2 px of a line centre, the error is the mean absolute HDR error against the 8x8 box filtered f64 truth over the last 8 frames of two jitter cycles. The bounds exceed the 8 to 10% lavapipe to Radeon spread of a ratio of two same-device runs.

**Known trade-off.** At 0.67 the clip makes the strip error 1.8% worse than dilation alone (0.03009 against 0.02956), still below the reset floor and inside the bound. A bar that moves 1 to 1.5 texels per frame is below what the clip resolves: the 3x3 slope term of its tolerance includes the dilated edge, so the texel directly behind the bar keeps confidence 1, and the clip fires only two or more texels away, where the 2x2 minimum gather takes history from a few texels that were still correct. A faster mover converges exactly. The clip was not tuned for this.

Receding, the new path removes more than half of the PR 2 excess over the reset floor in the ring and the edge band (asserted). Approaching, the new path is 1 to 20% worse than the PR 2 path in this scene and better than reset every frame (0.86 to 0.96 times). The PR 2 path is exact there because the bar is a uniform colour inside the checker range, so the YCoCg clip pulls any history inside it to the bar colour, and the new path rejects history at the growing edge where its depth changes, which costs the accumulated edge pixels. The asserted bound for the approach is therefore 1.2 times the reset error, not the PR 2 error: a surface that moves along the view axis gives up history at its changing edge and converges to the spatial reconstruction. A previous-depth or depth-delta channel in `velocity.rs` would remove that loss; it is not part of this change. Two overlapping movers in opposite directions behave as designed: the nearer one's motion fills the overlap and the texels next to it in the dilated motion, the farther one's uncovered texel has confidence 0, and no value is non-finite.

Policy and limits:

* Sky and texels with no valid depth have confidence 1 (see above). The same holds when the camera block has no depth terms.
* Masks and the water producer. A frame that reconstructs the output grid temporally owns one `Rg8Unorm` mask at the internal extent (`FrameTargets::temporal_mask_texture`, `TEMPORAL_MASK_FORMAT`): red is the reactive value, green the composition value, both in [0, 1]. The host clears it once per frame before any producer (`FrameTargets::encode_mask_clear`, first in the frame's encoder, so a frame whose encoder is dropped leaves nothing behind). Producers draw with `Max` blend on both channels and `Load`, declare the view with retained contents (the host clear is their producer), and must be 1x. A 3x3 per-channel max (`temporal mask dilate`) prepares the mask per slot; reactive scales the history weight by `1 - reactive`, composition above zero weakens the thin feature locks and at 1 removes them (locked entry point only). An absent mask schedules nothing and the output equals the path before masks. The water surface can produce a constant reactive value of 0.9 (policy constant, composition 0) through `FrameRenderer::set_water_reactive_mask`, which ships OFF: the water pass already writes four `Rgba16Float` outputs, the 32 byte colour attachment limit of a default device, so the producer is a second pass (`PassSlot::TransparentWaterMask`, after the water pass) that redraws the bodies with the water vertex stage into the mask only, `LessEqual` against the water pass's depth with depth writes off. Measured on lavapipe with stock water animating 0.1 s per frame, the producer did not meet the trail form (trail at most reset + 0.5 (zero mask - reset) at 0.5, 0.67 and 0.77): the accumulating run is already below the reset floor and the mask raises the error (0.00477 to 0.00788 at 0.67), so it stays off; the static region stays within 1.10x. Thin feature locks also ship off (see above, #436). Dynamic content that does not write a residual relies on the camera term and the depth clip alone.
* Motion coverage: terrain writes zero residual, which is correct. Water, dynamic quads, sky and celestial meshes write none, so they carry only the camera term. The depth clip rejects history where a covered surface moves in front of them, and nothing rejects history on a surface that moves in place.
* The dilate pass does not apply the jitter to the camera term (sub-pixel).
* All numbers are from static analytic scenes on lavapipe and, where stated, the Radeon 780M. GPU floating point results differ by 8 to 10% between the two, so every bound has a margin above that. The toward and away scene and the end-to-end camera tests were not run on the Radeon at the time of writing.

#### Material mip bias

The shipped material mip bias is exactly +0.0 in every mode, `Temporal` included. `HdrPostSettings::material_mip_bias(config)` returns +0.0, and the frame block carries it in its last f32 lane (bytes 316 to 320). The sampling sites are in place and tested, so a policy can be switched on with one change in `material_mip_bias`; none is active. The sites are the stock atlas sample, impostor and relief atlases with mips, decal and terrain gradients and layered terrain gradients. A nonzero bias would not touch explicit LOD 0 sampling, control fields (wind, flow), the BRDF lookup and environment roughness LOD, or the sky. Water normals stay unbiased: they are animated, and a finer mip mainly adds shimmer there. Shadow casters are drawn with a bias of exactly 0 (the light view is not reconstructed). A bias could not add terrain pages that are not resident; a page that was not streamed at the finer level would stay at the coarser level.

Why the policy is 0. The candidates were `log2(s) - 1`, `log2(s) - 0.5` and `log2(s)` with `s = min(Rw / Ow, Rh / Oh)` from the rounded integer extents. The first measurement, on lavapipe, compared each against the bias-0 reduced render and exceeded the original cap of 10% over its shimmer at all three scales. That cap compared against a render that is blurrier than native by construction, so the bar was changed after that first measurement to the shimmer of native-resolution TAA (scale 1.0, bias 0) on the same scene. The rule was then fixed before the second measurement: choose the most negative candidate whose shimmer is at most 1.10 times the native TAA shimmer and whose detail error is below the bias-0 error, at every scale 0.5, 0.67 and 0.77. A production quality claim has to hold on every tested device, and the rule is not changed a second time. On lavapipe `log2(s) - 0.5` passed. On the AMD Radeon 780M (RADV) no nonzero candidate passes at scale 0.5, so the policy is 0.

The stripe fixture (4 dark and 4 light output pixels per period, at the render Nyquist) is the worst case and is the one measured on both devices. A policy is then switched on in `material_mip_bias`, measured again with this harness (`crates/render-scene/src/draw/tests/mip_end_to_end.rs`, which prints the table) on both devices. Nothing else changes: existing settings and outputs are byte-identical.

## Windows, headless rendering and capture

`scene-viewer` presents a scene in a desktop window with up to three frames in flight, handles resize and minimize, and recovers from a lost surface. The same renderer can render headless to an offscreen target and read the pixels back, which is how the GPU tests check exact output bytes. A host can also schedule its own render passes alongside the stock ones, or read back GPU timings for every pass.
