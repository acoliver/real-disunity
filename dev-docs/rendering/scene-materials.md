# Scene materials and frame metadata

Scene inputs remain owned CPU data. TextureHandle, MaterialHandle and MeshHandle address the existing resource tables. Rendering does not resolve paths or decode application content.

SceneMaterial::with_features attaches typed tint, baked/vertex occlusion, wind and layer descriptions without rebuilding the base material. Layer weights and tint/occlusion factors use millionths. Layer references must address the same material table, and nested layers are rejected. SceneObject carries an independent instance tint. SceneMeshLod::with_card extends its existing billboard policy and rejects conflicting orientation policies.

Positions and card sizes use millimetres in the right-handed Z-up scene frame. Wind direction uses SceneDirection in that same frame, amplitude uses millimetres, period uses milliseconds, and phase is a fraction of a cycle in millionths. These descriptions perform no implicit basis changes. The existing view transform owns the rendering basis conversion.

SceneWaterBody owns one SceneWaterSurface: a rectangle, a regularly sampled basin bed, or authored planar triangle positions and indices. Basin samples cover the body's east/north bounds in row-major order. Each grid cell uses the southwest-to-northeast diagonal, and surface coverage ends where the linearly interpolated bed reaches the body elevation. Mesh positions are absolute east/north millimetres; the body supplies Z. Each body has its own elevation, scattering colour, absorption per metre, roughness and east/north flow in millimetres per second. All geometry and optical ranges are checked before GPU allocation.

SceneEffects::with_water_bodies replaces the complete body collection. The effects constructor's optional body is a single-body convenience input to that same collection. Empty collections leave the water feature disabled. The existing water pass reads immutable opaque snapshots for all bodies and writes the engine-owned depth attachment. Depth testing selects the nearest surface independently of body order. Per-body frame uniforms retain three isolated slots, and resize rebuilds the surfaces and snapshots together.

Each SceneWaterBody::material carries SceneWaterSettings with optional normal and flow TextureHandles into the existing scene texture table. SceneEffects::with_water_settings applies the same material inputs to every current body. Maps must be linear RG8, RGBA8, BGRA8 or BC5 data; unsupported formats and sRGB maps fail before scene allocation. Uploaded views are reused, without uploading a second copy. Normal RG decodes from [0, 1] to signed east/north components, reconstructing positive Z and normalizing. Flow RG decodes to signed east/north velocity, scaled by flow_speed_millimeters_per_second and added to the body's constant velocity. Coordinates repeat in world east/north space with independently authored, positive tile_millimeters extents. Normal coordinates scroll by the resulting velocity times frame seconds; spatial flow is sampled at the unscrolled world coordinates. Flow uses linear filtering at mip zero. With `normal_mips`, normals use derivative-selected trilinear sampling and `normal_anisotropy` sets the validated maximum footprint ratio (1, 2, 4, 8 or 16). The default is 1; equal levels share a sampler. wgpu clamps anisotropy to 1 on adapters without anisotropic filtering support. Unless the body selects normal mips, the shader computes that bilinear filter in float precision from texel loads with repeat wrapping, because Vulkan leaves sampler filter precision implementation-defined and some software samplers quantize 8-bit weights and results. Missing normal maps explicitly select procedural waves; missing flow maps select constant body velocity. A missing refraction bound (`None`) preserves stock screen-clamped refraction. Every authored bound (`Some`), including zero, is rejected until displacement limiting is implemented.

`SceneWaterFlowCoordinates` supplies an independent integer-millimetre seed origin and positive domain extent (`tile_millimeters`). Its `mode` defaults to `SceneWaterSeedCoordinates::Repeat`, which preserves repeat-wrapped sampling and default uniform bytes. `EndpointClamped` treats the domain as an endpoint-inclusive lattice: for normalized position `t = (world - origin) / extent`, each axis maps to `uv = (clamp(t, 0, 1) * (N - 1) + 0.5) / N`. The shader bilinearly filters mip-zero texels in float precision and clamps outside the domain. The first and last texels lie at the minimum and maximum bounds. This mode requires a flow map with at least two samples per axis, checked before GPU allocation, and non-zero domain extents. CPU geometry subtracts the integer domain origin before narrowing normalized seed coordinates to float32. An endpoint-only vertex specialization interpolates those coordinates, with no absolute-world subtraction in the fragment shader. Repeat keeps its vertex layout, shader arithmetic and uniform bytes; normal maps retain their independent repeat coordinates and filtering. The asymmetric 17×17 synthetic seed test compares GPU output against an independent clamped lattice oracle at endpoints, near edges, outside bounds and in the interior. Translated mesh, rectangle and basin tests cover large positive and negative origins, including a 50 mm offset in a 100 mm domain, with a tolerance derived from float32 arithmetic and float16 storage.


`SceneWaterSettings::flow_seed_sampling` is `SceneWaterSeedSampling::Fragment` by default. `Vertex` requires a resident flow map and composes with either seed address mode. It samples raw RG unorm seeds at the emitted rectangle, clipped basin or indexed mesh vertices. The shared bilinear addressing functions keep their float-precision weights. Vertex fetches texel centres using `textureSampleLevel` at explicit mip zero, avoiding quantized fractional filter weights on downlevel samplers. These vertex texture reads use the same capabilities as vertex-sampled wind. A perspective-correct sample-frequency varying carries raw seeds; signed decoding, wind shaping and shoreline depth shaping remain in the fragment stage. No CPU seed cache or additional persistent buffer is allocated, and endpoint coordinate storage uses the same sizing helper in allocation and planning. Fragment retains the original shader source and bytes. The independent tent-basis CPU oracle and tiny GPU raster tests check a 17×17 asymmetric lattice on the triangle (0,0), (0.6,0), (0,0.6) metres in a 10 m tile. Its centroid differs from sampling the centroid directly by 0.002811 raw unorm in red. Tests also cover green, both address modes, out-of-domain clamping, corners, independently addressed tile edges and nonuniform clip W.

FrameRenderer::set_water_environment and ConfiguredFrameRenderer::set_water_environment accept submitted PreparedEnvironmentMaps from sky::preparation. The configured wrapper retains those host-budgeted views during replacement, including extent changes, and exposes submitted water_receivers without unrestricted renderer access. Water samples the prefiltered cube at perceptual roughness times the last mip and the BRDF LUT at (normal dot view, roughness), with dielectric F0 0.02037. Missing environment maps contribute zero specular radiance. Shared hemisphere and light-direction kernels and the existing cascaded shadow binding supply water lighting. Water carries the scene direct-diffuse convention through its plain and shadowed pipeline cache and shared shader constants. `LambertianPrepared` consumes prepared solar colour without an extra Fresnel or pi diffuse factor. Per-body sun-disk controls remain independent of the scene surface disk. Default lighting and 1x normal filtering preserve output bytes. The height-fog kernel and compose_water_fog interface preserve the opaque snapshot's existing fog and apply the camera segment only to newly added scattering and reflection. No division by fog transmittance is needed, including fully opaque fog.

## Rendering support

Stock rendering supports material and instance tint, baked and optional vertex AO, rigid periodic wind translation, thin-surface transmission, card dimensions and mesh/card transitions. The frame owns `animation_time_milliseconds`; all geometry passes consume the same clock. Card fitting inverse-transposes authored normals before the placement transform.

Transition draws compare one coverage fraction against a shared integer hash of fragment pixel coordinates. Mesh and card acceptance is exclusive and complete wherever their rasterized silhouettes overlap, independently of their UV interpolation. Outside that overlap each representation retains its own silhouette and alpha cutouts. Every pass uses the same function; shadow pixels belong to the shadow view's raster grid.

Terrain contact blends toward authored constant linear albedo, roughness and metalness using the selected terrain geometry's triangle heights, including shortened final cells. It does not sample the terrain material pages. Contact retains only selected height samples and validates buffer and storage binding limits before allocation. Scenes with no contact material bind a shared inactive 48-byte scene buffer. Arbitrary layers, geometry features on terrain palette materials and authored water refraction bounds remain explicitly rejected before upload.

The existing mesh and billboard vertex transform is reused by forward, shadow and metadata passes. Their masked fragments call one alpha-coverage predicate. Terrain remains opaque. Water writes surface depth and per-sample normal, material and environment-specular receivers in the same raster operation. Dynamic transparent quads remain outside the opaque metadata prepass. Adding a producer requires matching its deformation, coverage and numerical products, rather than substituting metadata from a different geometry path.

## Shared products

FrameProduct describes an existing executor ResourceRef, numerical meaning, texture format and sampling domain. It does not allocate textures or mint another resource identity. SSAO prepass, reflection material and water-depth snapshot declarations use these descriptions. Shared source validation requires matching extent and sampling, D2 single-layer textures and sampled-texture usage.

| Product | Representation | Coverage and units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prepass depth | Depth32Float | Device depth, cleared to one |
| Prepass normal | Rgba16Float | View-space unit normal RGB, coverage alpha; uncovered alpha zero |
| Reflection material | Rgba16Float | Linear F0 RGB, post-decal material roughness channel in alpha before lighting remap or lower clamp |
| Conservative depth | R32Float | Nearest positive view depth in metres |
| Averaged depth/normal | Rgba16Float | Separate declared reduction meaning, not a traversal input |
| Environment specular | Rgba16Float | Linear HDR replaceable specular radiance, not total shaded colour |

Averaged occlusion metadata is a separate reduction product. Opaque environment specular remains an explicit integration point; water publishes its own replaceable specular lobe. Pixel-centre prepass coverage remains distinct from forward MSAA coverage. Per-sample source products permit only 1x and 4x. Forward depth snapshots declare conservative resolution with the actual 1x or 4x source count and one stored sample; pixel-centre hierarchy depth retains its separate sampling policy. Reduced depth products must be single-sample. Formats with sRGB transfer functions are rejected for metadata and specular radiance.

Water adds three named Rgba16Float outputs in the TransparentWater pass, without changing StageIds. `water receiver normals` stores view-space unit normal RGB and a water flag in alpha: one for covered water samples and zero elsewhere. `water receiver material` stores linear dielectric F0 RGB and perceptual roughness alpha. `water environment specular` stores the fog-attenuated, replaceable environment lobe RGB, excluding absorption, scattering and background. All three share the forward depth attachment's 1x or 4x sampling and visibility, are cleared to zero, and retain three independent admission slots. They are not resolved pixel-centre metadata. R9 consumers must fetch the matching sample, rather than average normal, material or water flags. FrameRenderer::water_receivers exposes the submitted slot's views, and clears readiness on slot selection and resize. Resize retains installed environment maps. Capture declarations and allocation preflight include all receiver storage.

Stock resource names, frame stages and allocation/publication ownership remain unchanged. Water-disabled rendering retains its previous bytes and pass schedule. Metadata declarations acquire no independent publication lifecycle. Cancellation uses the existing prepared-frame transaction and never submits its encoded writes.
