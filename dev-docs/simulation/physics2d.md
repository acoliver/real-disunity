# Native 2D physics

`worldrunner::simulation::World` (crate `worldrunner-world-simulation`) owns physics alongside its object and component arenas. Add `ComponentDesc::Body2d` and `ComponentDesc::Collider2d` to existing objects. Rust applications need no managed runtime, renderer or serialization dependency.

There are two ways to step physics. `World::advance` runs physics inside each admitted fixed step, as described in [frame advancement](advancement.md); this is how scripted worlds and the managed host run. `World::step_physics(delta_seconds)` steps once directly, for native callers that own their own fixed-step schedule. `World::step_with_edits` stages a batch of typed `ObjectEdit`s and one physics step in a temporary copy and publishes both together. A failed step does not publish body, transform or contact changes, and a failed `step_with_edits` leaves the edits with the caller for resubmission.

## Coordinates and shapes

`PhysicsSettings` selects `PhysicsPlane::Xy` or `PhysicsPlane::Xz`, gravity and an iteration budget. Configure it with `World::set_physics_settings`. Distances are metres and angles, including angular velocity, are radians. Plane methods convert positions, directions and rotations explicitly. XY is the default plane. XZ projects native X and Z onto the two physics axes, so negative second-axis gravity points down in the engine's Z-up coordinates.

Static bodies do not integrate. Kinematic bodies follow prescribed velocity and do not receive impulses. Dynamic bodies integrate gravity and receive contact impulses according to mass and restitution. Frozen rotation suppresses prescribed angular velocity and angular impulses. `Body2d::freeze_position` (X = 1, Y = 2) removes linear motion on that physics axis from integration, contact impulses, movement targets and continuous collision. A translation-only body keeps its stored quaternion unchanged unless an independently rotating parent requires local counter-rotation.

A collider contains one or more boxes, circles or strictly convex polygons. The nearest ancestor body owns child colliders, and colliders attached to the same body cannot collide with one another. Colliders without a body are static. A static collider beneath an independently simulated ancestor body is rejected with `PhysicsError::UnsupportedHierarchy` before advancement; static bodies do not inherit simulated parent motion. Collider geometry uses the full object world matrix. Nonplanar, singular or numerically collapsed computed geometry fails. Boxes and polygons must retain representable nonzero edges and area after world transformation. Circle intersections use double-precision products and a cancellation-resistant root calculation, including ray and vertex queries. A transformed circle must remain a circle; nonuniform or sheared circle transforms fail rather than approximate an ellipse.

A collider's `ColliderGeometry` is either its own `Shapes` or `Tilemap`, Unity's `TilemapCollider2D`. A tilemap collider rebuilds its shapes each step from the `world-simulation::tilemap::Tilemap` on the same object, so cell edits change what collides without rewriting the collider; with no Tilemap it has no shapes. Each cell contributes by its tile's collider type: Grid fills the cell, Sprite uses the sprite's physics shape (convex paths around the pivot) moved to the cell's anchored point and transformed with the tile, and None contributes nothing. Tracing a sprite's outline from its alpha is the importer's job; native code receives the paths. Cell coordinates are bounded to signed 16-bit values and must be distinct; a description allows at most 4,096 shapes, with 8,192 world shapes. No missing shape is replaced with a rectangle.

Continuous translational collision uses convex sweeps, including circle-edge and circle-vertex hits. Angular motion uses conservative advancement bounded by separating-axis clearance, projected point velocities and rotational acceleration. It evaluates the angular trajectory between poses and resolves at the impact pose for both offset circles and convex bodies. The solver fails with `PhysicsError::IterationLimit` if the configured budget cannot complete a step. It does not silently discard the remaining delta. Joints and concave shapes are outside this subset.

## Shared surface materials

Create a validated `PhysicsMaterial2d` with `PhysicsMaterial2d::new(friction, bounciness)` and register it with `World::create_physics_material`. Friction must be finite and nonnegative; bounciness must be finite and between zero and one. Invalid coefficients return `PhysicsError::InvalidMaterial`. The returned `PhysicsMaterialHandle` belongs to that world. Assign it to `Collider2d::shared_material` or `Body2d::shared_material`. Foreign-world references fail before component mutation. Assets are bounded by the world's asset limit and released on world unload.

The collider's asset overrides the nearest attached body's asset. `World::collider_material` reports the resolved coefficients. `World::set_physics_material` changes a shared asset for every referencing collider, including cloned components. If neither component has an asset, friction stays zero and the body's existing restitution still applies. This preserves existing native behavior rather than introducing Unity's global default friction.

Contacts use the geometric mean of friction and the maximum bounciness. Only these default combine rules are exposed; selectable combine modes are not implemented. Material-backed contacts apply restitution only when closing normal speed exceeds `PhysicsSettings::bounce_threshold`, which defaults to 1 metre per second and must be finite and nonnegative. Contacts without either material use the body's own `restitution` at every speed. The solver applies a tangential impulse limited by friction times the normal impulse, including contact-point angular velocity and inertia. A rotation-frozen body on an incline stops when friction exceeds the tangent of the slope angle and slides below that threshold. Bounciness one produces an elastic normal response; zero removes closing normal velocity.

`Disunity.Engine` exposes `UnityEngine.PhysicsMaterial2D`, `Collider2D.sharedMaterial`, and `Rigidbody2D.sharedMaterial` through generated ABI exports. New managed assets start with friction 0.4 and bounciness zero. Reading sharedMaterial returns the explicit asset reference or null, not the resolved fallback. Ordinary component property writes retain their material reference.

Unity documents [collider-over-body precedence](https://docs.unity3d.com/ScriptReference/Rigidbody2D-sharedMaterial.html), [default friction and bounce mixing](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/2d-physics/physics-material-2d-reference.html), the [geometric-mean definition](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.5/Documentation/Manual/2d-physics-api/2d-physics-api-reference-shape.html), and the [bounce velocity threshold](https://docs.unity3d.com/ScriptReference/Physics2D-bounceThreshold.html).

## Contacts and queries

Each successful step replaces `World::contacts()` with typed `ContactEvent` observations sorted by collider identity pair and then phase. Persistent overlaps produce enter, stay and exit. A trigger swept completely through another collider produces enter and exit within the same step. Contact identities are generation-checked component handles; events also identify objects and attached bodies. Normals point from A to B in the selected plane. Exit observations preserve the identities of the ended pair, even after retirement.

Use `World::next_contact()` for consuming delivery. Before returning an event, it checks both colliders, both attached bodies and active hierarchy state. Disabling or destroying an endpoint during dispatch suppresses its remaining records. Exit observations for ended contacts remain available through `contacts()`. A pending destroyed endpoint stops receiving contact callbacks at once; its handles stay valid until end-of-frame cleanup retires the storage, and reuse cannot revive old handles.

`raycast`, `overlap_point` and `overlap_shape` read the same world geometry. `QueryFilter` selects layers and trigger inclusion. Ray hits are sorted by distance, with collider identity breaking ties; compound shapes yield one closest hit per collider. Overlap results are unique collider handles in deterministic order. Collider masks and the symmetric world layer matrix govern simulation pairs. Query masks are independent of the simulation matrix.
