# Ambient occlusion and scene metadata

`SceneSsao` selects sampling and reconstruction independently. Radius and bias are authored in millimetres and converted once to view-space metres. Strength uses micro-units. The horizon angle limits directions relative to the receiving normal.

`SceneSsaoKernel::Hemisphere` retains the full-resolution projected hemisphere sampler. `SceneSsaoKernel::Spiral` samples a deterministic golden-angle screen-space pattern with squared radial spacing and pixel-dependent rotation. Sample distance chooses among averaged metadata footprints. Samples outside the screen, outside the radius, behind the receiving normal or without geometry coverage contribute no occlusion. Both policies return visibility in `[0, 1]`, with one meaning unoccluded. The ambient-only specular convention applies AO to ambient diffuse; the roughness-aware convention also uses it for specular occlusion and directional relief.

`SceneSsaoFilter::DepthAwareBox` retains the existing five-by-five depth-aware tent. `SceneSsaoFilter::SeparableBilateral` runs horizontal and vertical nine-tap Gaussian passes. Taps are spaced two pixels apart. The Gaussian standard deviation is `4 / 1.5` taps, and the depth weight is `exp(-7 * abs(depth_difference_metres))`. Samples at least one metre apart are excluded. Each pass divides by its accepted weight sum, preserving constant visibility without an added brightness multiplier. Texture boundaries replicate their edge texel.

## Shared source, distinct reductions

`scene_metadata` owns source textures and geometry pipeline preparation. The normal and material prepasses use the forward geometry shader's deformation, material normals and alpha-coverage predicates, including foliage animation and transitions. Normals are in view space, with alpha zero for uncovered pixels. Depth linearization produces positive view depth, not radial camera distance.

The source prepass uses pixel-centre coverage at both 1x and 4x forward MSAA. It is deliberately not an MSAA coverage resolve. Source metadata consumers share this sampling domain, while resolved forward-depth products retain their separate coverage contract.

The conservative `R32Float` hierarchy remains a minimum reduction for reflection traversal. Shadow depth statistics read full-resolution linear depth and normal coverage. A checked same-frame prefix supplies these products to the remaining screen passes without another geometry prepass or full-resolution linearization. Neither consumer reads the averaged AO hierarchy.

Spiral sampling adds a separate `FrameProduct::occlusion_metadata(level)` hierarchy with `AverageDepthNormalMeters` meaning. RGB stores the averaged view-normal vector and alpha stores average positive view depth in metres. Zero alpha denotes no covered input. Covered inputs are averaged over a two-by-two footprint with edge replication at odd extents. Normal vectors are not renormalized after reduction, preserving the coherence of the averaged directions. The hierarchy stops at level six or 1x1, whichever comes first; sampling binds levels zero, two, four and six, clamped to the final level. This half-float product has half-float depth precision and range; it must not be used as conservative traversal depth or shadow statistics.

The existing pass names and stages remain unchanged for the hemisphere and box policies. Only the explicit new policies add averaged-metadata or horizontal-filter passes. Configured renderer allocation budgets include all new textures, and transactional replacement retires their resources with the rest of the frame configuration.

## Full-resolution receivers

`SceneSsao.receiver` accepts `SceneSsaoReceiver::Nearest` (the default) or `DepthAwareGather`. Nearest loads one filtered AO texel and leaves shader text, uniform bytes, pipeline keys and material composition unchanged. Gather loads the four texels around `uv * ao_extent - 0.5`, replicating texture edges. It excludes nonpositive tap depths from the clamp bounds and weights, clamps positive receiver view depth to the covered taps' range, weights each covered tap by `exp(-abs(receiver_depth - tap_depth))` in metres, and normalizes. An entirely uncovered footprint returns visibility one before material composition. A common exponent offset prevents underflow without changing the normalized weights. There are no spatial interpolation weights.

The last AO filter writes visibility and its source depth into `Rg32Float` only for gather. Hemisphere uses full-resolution linear view depth. Spiral uses the half-rounded alpha of level-zero occlusion metadata, including zero for uncovered pixels. Scalable AO uses `FrameProduct::averaged_depth(0)`, the half-resolution depth consumed by its AO pass, rather than conservative minimum depth or normalized half-float depth. Hemisphere retains positive far-plane depth at uncovered pixels. Scalable averages positive source depths, including uncovered far-plane samples, and checks normal-alpha coverage at its selected full-resolution centre. Both kernels produce neutral raw AO for an uncovered centre without a zero-depth sentinel. Only Spiral metadata encodes absent geometry as zero depth. Receiver view depth uses the existing screen-camera buffer. Meshes, cached and layered terrain, shadow receivers, reflection receivers and term captures share the same receiver specialization. With depth-relative application AO enabled, SSR resolve uses the same gather function, the full-resolution receiver depth and the packed metre depths. Native AO captures decode red; capture admission accounts for the eight-byte gather texel. Ambient diffuse and roughness-aware specular occlusion consume `min(1.01 * gathered_ao, material_ao)` before the existing directional relief. Nearest retains multiplication under the ambient-only convention and the existing minimum under roughness-aware occlusion.

Gather widens only the final AO target by four bytes per AO texel, charged to configuration budgets. It adds no texture, sampler, pass or uniform allocation and no sampled-texture binding, preserving the 16-texture baseline. Binding 5 of the existing screen group reuses the screen-camera uniform.
